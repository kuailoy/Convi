# Finnish word-form list — pilot draft

> Scope: the first three Topics in the source learning order. These are compact A1–A2 learning targets, not complete paradigms. Review forms and usage before treating them as validated or importing them. Words without productive inflection are explicitly marked; some relational words instead govern the case of a neighboring word.

## Review notes

- `ilman` is a preposition and `varten` takes a partitive complement; most listed postpositions take genitive complements. Some relational postpositions have their own locative forms (for example `välissä / välistä / väliin`). Personal pronoun complements commonly take possessive suffixes on the postposition (`minun kanssani`).
- Corrections from the first draft: `joku` takes `jollekulle` (not `jollekin`) in the allative paradigm; `itse` has ordinary case forms (`itsen`, `itseä`) as well as possessive-suffix forms; `ilman` takes a partitive complement and precedes it; `varten` also takes a partitive complement; `välillä` has related forms `välissä / välistä / väliin`; `esiin / esille` belong to a directional form family, so they are not best labeled simply “invariable.”
- Most items in “Questions and connectors” are invariant. `ei` is the notable conjugating negative auxiliary; `juuri` and `tosi` have different behavior depending on part of speech/meaning.
- “Relational words” includes postpositions. Their learning target is often the case of the preceding complement, not a declension of the postposition itself.
- Keep standard written Finnish as the default; colloquial forms can be a separate later layer.
